Our research asks how speakers produce and perceive the sounds of Spanish, how
pronunciation varies across speakers, dialects and communities, and how
bilingual speakers combine Spanish and English when they speak and write. Part
of this work focuses on heritage speakers: people who grew up with Spanish at
home in an English-dominant society.

To answer these questions we record speech and analyze it acoustically, run
production experiments in the lab and online, and build annotated corpora of
spoken and written language. Alongside this research we develop computational
tools, from acoustic analysis scripts to language identification and annotation
pipelines, so that linguistic analysis becomes faster, more transparent and
easier to reproduce. We work mainly with Praat, R and Python.

## Our approach

**Language as people use it.** We study speech and writing as they occur in
real communities, and we describe bilingual speakers' language as a full
repertoire rather than measuring it against a monolingual norm.

**Open and reproducible.** We share data, code and methods so that others can
check our work and build on it.

**Students as researchers.** Undergraduates join the lab as paid interns and
take part in every stage of a project, from collecting data to testing tools.
See the [MOSAICO internship]({{ '/internship/' | relative_url }}).
