## Why MOSAICO

Heritage speakers of Spanish grew up with Spanish at home in an
English-dominant society. When they write, they draw on everything they know
in both languages: they move between Spanish and English, use structures
shaped by contact with English, and bring in the vocabulary of their families'
regional varieties.

Most computational tools for analyzing writing were designed for second-language
learners or for monolingual writers, and they treat departures from a
monolingual standard as errors. MOSAICO starts from a different place.

## Approach

MOSAICO works within a linguistic repertoire framework. Rather than measuring
heritage speakers against a monolingual norm, or tracking how close they come
to it, the toolkit describes the resources a writer uses and how those
resources are combined.

## Modules

The toolkit is built as six modules. Each semester, a cohort of undergraduate
interns develops and evaluates the next part of the toolkit, building on the
work of the cohort before.

## Data

The project begins with a pilot corpus of writing samples contributed by UNR
students who grew up with Spanish. Participation is voluntary, and samples are
collected with informed consent.
